Writing and using moves ​

A move is a Metropolis–Hastings transition on the latent point of a posterior. A correct move leaves the posterior invariant, so it can be interleaved with the transitions of any MCMC sampler that also leaves the posterior invariant. Moves target directions that gradient samplers explore slowly, for example a degeneracy between the total flux and the gain amplitudes, the 2π ambiguity of a gain phase, or the hyperparameters of a gain chain against the chain values.

What a move does ​

A move acts on the base latent point x of the posterior, in one of two spaces:

  • the flat space of asflat, or

  • the standard normal space of transport_to(post, StdNormal()).

Given x, a move draws a step from its step distribution and proposes a point x′ = propose(move, x, step, ctx). The proposal is accepted with probability min(1, α),

Using moves ​

Built-in moves ​

Parameters are addressed by their path, a tuple of field names such as (:sky, :f1) or (:instrument, :lg). A CoordinateView maps paths to latent coordinates in a chosen space. Comrade provides these moves:

  • flux_gain_move: trades a scalar flux parameter against the gain log-amplitudes, keeping F · exp(power · g) fixed;

  • PhaseSheetMove: adds ±2π to one site's path of a real-line Gauss–Markov phase chain, from a free point up to the next point the model fixes;

  • chain_hyper_moves: one ChainHyperMove per fitted hyperparameter of a Gauss–Markov chain term, which updates the hyperparameter of each site while keeping the chain values fixed;

  • CompensatedMove: a general random-walk move that shifts one latent coordinate and recomputes a block of parameters to compensate (see Writing a move).

Building a MoveSet ​

A MoveSet holds the moves, their step scales and their statistics:

julia
using Comrade

sp = StdNormal()
view = CoordinateView(post, sp)
fg = flux_gain_move(view; flux = (:sky, :f1), gains = (:instrument, :lg))
sheet = PhaseSheetMove(post, (:gp,); space = sp)
hyper = chain_hyper_moves(view, (:instrument, :lg))

moves = (fg, sheet, hyper...)
ms = MoveSet(
    post, moves; space = sp, θ0 = prior_sample(post),
    rounds = [5; 3; fill(2, length(hyper))],
    initial_scale = [0.05; nothing; fill(0.5, length(hyper))],
)

The options are per move. A number applies to every move and a vector gives one value per move.

  • rounds: proposals of each move per call.

  • initial_scale: the starting step scale τ of moves with a NormalStep or ComponentwiseNormalStep. It is ignored for other moves, so their entries may be nothing.

  • target_accept: the acceptance rate each step scale is adapted toward during warmup (default 0.45).

  • θ0: a constrained parameter point. If given, construction checks there that every move flagged likelihood_invariant leaves the likelihood unchanged, and that the components of every componentwise move are independent.

  • rng: the random number generator for the steps of the θ0 checks.

  • output: a file path. If given, move_summary is serialized there after every call.

The moves, the MoveSet, and the sampler must all use the same latent space. A call throws an error otherwise.

With any sampler ​

Call the MoveSet on the latent point between the sampler's steps:

julia
using Random

function run_chain(ms, tpost, x, rng; nwarmup, nsamples)
    for i in 1:nwarmup
        x = my_sampler_step(x)
        x = ms(x, tpost, :warmup, rng; step = i, total = nwarmup, report = i % 100 == 0)
    end
    for i in 1:nsamples
        x = my_sampler_step(x)
        x = ms(x, tpost, :sampling, rng)
    end
    return x
end

tpost = transport_to(post, sp)
x = run_chain(ms, tpost, prior_sample(tpost), Random.default_rng(); nwarmup = 1000, nsamples = 1000)

tpost must sample in the space of the MoveSet. The call returns x itself when no proposal was accepted, and a new array otherwise; a sampler that caches the log density or gradient at x must recompute them when x′ !== x. Each call makes rounds[j] proposals of move j. Traceable moves run first, then the others, each group interleaved round by round.

The phase controls adaptation. During :warmup the log step scale of each scaled move (and of each component of a componentwise move) follows a Robbins–Monro recursion toward target_accept. During :sampling the scales are fixed. A warmup call with report = true logs a table of acceptance rates and step scales over the calls since the last table, labeled with step out of total.

With ReactantNUTS ​

Pass the MoveSet to sample as between_chunks, and request the same latent space with transport_method:

julia
using Reactant

out = sample(
    post_device, ReactantNUTS(; n_adapts = 2000), 2000;
    transport_method = sp, between_chunks = ms,
    saveto = DiskStore(name = "Results", stride = 50),
).out

Build the MoveSet from the host posterior post; post_device may be its device copy. The moves run after every warmup chunk (warmup_chunk, by default the DiskStore stride or chunk_size) and after every sampling chunk except the last, so the chunk length sets how often they run. The chain stores only the NUTS draws. When the moves change the position, the next chunk recomputes the gradient and keeps the step size, metric, and adaptation state.

With a device position, moves that are traceable propose inside one compiled Reactant program per call, and the position stays on the device. Other moves propose on the host from a copy of the position, with ctx.view over the host posterior the MoveSet was built from rather than the device posterior; their log density is evaluated on the device.

The method ms(state, tpost, info, rng) is the between_chunks form used by ReactantNUTS. It updates state.position and reads phase, step, total, and report from the NamedTuple info.

Reading the statistics ​

julia
for s in move_summary(ms)
    rate = s.sampling.accepted / s.sampling.proposed
    println(s.name, ": τ = ", s.τ, ", sampling acceptance = ", rate)
end
move_seconds(ms)   # total wall time spent in the moves

Each entry of move_summary has name, the current step scale τ (nothing for a move without one, a vector for a componentwise move), rounds, the proposed and accepted counts in warmup and sampling, and bystep, a dictionary of label => [proposed, accepted] keyed by step_label (or by component_label for a componentwise move).

Writing a move ​

A move is a subtype of AbstractMove that implements three methods:

  • propose(move, x, step, ctx): the proposal from the latent point x;

  • step_distribution(move): one of the three step distributions below;

  • move_name(move): a name for logs and statistics, unique within a MoveSet.

These functions are not exported, so extend them as Comrade.propose and so on.

The step distribution determines the step that propose receives and what it must return:

Step distributionstep passed to proposepropose returnsReverse step
NormalStep()scalar u ~ N(0, τ²)(x′, logdet)-u
ComponentwiseNormalStep(n)vector u with uᵢ ~ N(0, τᵢ²)(x′, logdets), one per component-u
SymmetricDiscreteStep(draw, reverse)draw(rng)(x′, logdet)reverse(step)

Here logdet = log|det ∂x′/∂x|. Proposing with the reverse step from x′ must return x with log-determinant -logdet. propose must not modify x.

A componentwise move also implements component_coords(move), the latent coordinates each component changes. These sets must be disjoint, and component i of the proposal must depend only on uᵢ and the coordinates of component i, so that each component is accepted on its own. A componentwise move must be likelihood invariant.

The argument ctx is a NamedTuple. ctx.view is a CoordinateView over the posterior being sampled; use it to read and write parameters by path:

  • coords(ctx.view, path): the latent coordinates of a parameter;

  • value(ctx.view, x, path): its value at x;

  • latent(ctx.view, path, v): the latent coordinates that give it the value v.

The remaining entries of ctx are the arrays returned by the moves' context_data.

Optional methods:

  • likelihood_invariant(move) (default false): declares that the move leaves the likelihood unchanged, which enables acceptance without the likelihood.

  • step_label(move, step) (default nothing): a key under which proposals and acceptances of step are counted in bystep.

  • component_label(move, i) (default i): the label of component i of a componentwise move.

  • context_data(move, view) (default empty): arrays the move reads from ctx. update_context! overwrites them in place, including the copies used by a compiled device program.

  • traceable(move) (default false): declares that propose compiles with Reactant. A traceable move has a NormalStep and reads every array it needs from ctx instead of capturing host objects.

A compensated move ​

Suppose the sky model depends on two parameters a and b only through their product a * b. A CompensatedMove can random-walk the latent coordinate of a and set b′ = b a / a′, which leaves the likelihood unchanged:

julia
view = CoordinateView(post, sp)
ab = CompensatedMove(
    "a_b", view, (:sky, :a), (:sky, :b),
    (vb, x, x′, ctx) -> vb * Comrade.value(ctx.view, x, (:sky, :a)) /
        Comrade.value(ctx.view, x′, (:sky, :a));
    likelihood_invariant = true,
)

The function compensate(vb, x, x′, ctx) receives the value vb of the block at x, and x′, which is x with only the shift applied. It returns the new value of the block. With log-normal priors on a and b, the latent coordinate of each is an affine function of its logarithm in both spaces, so the latent map is a translation and the default logdet = 0 is correct. Otherwise pass logdet = (vb, x, x′, ctx) -> ..., which receives the complete proposal x′.

A custom move ​

If the likelihood depends on a phase φ only through cos φ and sin φ, and φ has an unbounded prior such as a normal distribution, then shifts by ±2π leave the likelihood unchanged:

julia
struct PhaseWrap{P <: Tuple} <: Comrade.AbstractMove
    path::P
end

Comrade.step_distribution(::PhaseWrap) = SymmetricDiscreteStep(rng -> rand(rng, (-1, 1)), -)
Comrade.move_name(::PhaseWrap) = "phase_wrap"
Comrade.likelihood_invariant(::PhaseWrap) = true

function Comrade.propose(m::PhaseWrap, x, s, ctx)
    x′ = copy(x)
    φ = Comrade.value(ctx.view, x, m.path)
    x′[Comrade.coords(ctx.view, m.path)] = Comrade.latent(ctx.view, m.path, φ + 2π * s)
    return x′, 0.0
end

move = PhaseWrap((:sky, :φ))

The step s = ±1 is reversed by -s. For a normal prior the latent coordinate is an affine function of φ in both spaces, so the log-determinant is zero.

Checking a move ​

Run check_move on every new move before using it in a sampler. It takes the move, the posterior, and a collection of constrained parameter points, and throws an error that names the first failed property:

julia
θs = [prior_sample(post) for _ in 1:3]
r = check_move(move, post, θs; space = sp, τ = 0.1, nprior = 400, ngeweke = 1000, geweke_steps = 20)

At each point in θs it checks:

  • reversal: the reverse step from the proposal returns to the start, with the opposite log-determinant;

  • log-determinant: logdet agrees with a central finite-difference Jacobian (step h) over the coordinates the proposal changes;

  • invariance: a move flagged likelihood_invariant leaves the log-likelihood unchanged;

  • components: for a componentwise move, the components are independent.

Two statistical tests are optional:

  • nprior > 0: a prior-invariance test. nprior prior draws each take nsteps Metropolis–Hastings steps of the move targeting the prior, and are compared with fresh prior draws on up to ncoords changed coordinates by two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests at level ks_alpha.

  • ngeweke > 0: Geweke's (2004) joint-distribution test of the kernel that MoveSet runs. Each replicate draws θ from the prior and data from p(y | θ) (simulate_observation), then alternates geweke_steps move steps targeting p(θ | y) with fresh draws of the data. The kernel leaves the posterior invariant if and only if the results remain draws of the joint distribution p(θ, y). The replicates are compared with fresh joint draws on the changed coordinates, on the projection onto the move direction, and on the log-likelihood. This test requires a posterior whose data can be simulated, which excludes image data.

Steps are drawn at scale τ.

The result reports the largest reversal and log-determinant errors, the largest log-likelihood change, the KS statistics (ks, geweke) with their critical values, and the acceptance rate in the joint test (geweke_accept). Check that geweke_accept is not close to zero: a move that rarely moves passes the joint test without being tested.

check_moveset runs the joint-distribution test on the kernel of a whole MoveSet, at its current step scales and rounds:

julia
r = check_moveset(ms; ngeweke = 1000, geweke_steps = 20)
all(<=(r.geweke_critical), r.geweke)

It returns geweke, geweke_critical, and geweke_accept (one rate per move), and throws an error if a statistic exceeds its critical value.
